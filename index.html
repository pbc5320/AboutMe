About

Security, conflict, and the language that frames them

I am a first-year PhD student in the Department of Political Science at Penn State, advised by Dr. Jared Edgerton. I study why negotiations fail, how states use repression and violence, and how evaluative meaning spreads through language, using quantitative and computational methods. I hold a B.S. in Security and Risk Analysis and a Master of International Affairs (Middle Eastern Studies), both from Penn State.

Current Focus Areas

Nuclear Negotiations

Why do some nuclear bargaining efforts collapse? My thesis tests whether perceived issue indivisibility matters beyond regime type and power asymmetry.

Domestic Politics and War

How domestic institutions and political incentives shape decisions to fight, bargain, or settle.

Government Repression

How governments use coercion against their own populations, and what that means for stability and violence.

Political Violence

Cross-national work on autocratic governance and the lethality of terrorism across more than 100 countries.

State Failure

The conditions under which state capacity erodes, and how that connects to conflict and insecurity.

Semantic Prosody

Building a Python tool that detects the evaluative meaning words pick up from their company, applied to historical and contemporary text.
