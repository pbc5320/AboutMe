Research

My work asks how bargaining, coercion, and language shape conflict and security outcomes.

Research agenda

Themes

Bargaining, negotiation, and war

I study the conditions under which negotiations succeed or fail, with a focus on nuclear issues. I ask whether how parties perceive an issue, as divisible or indivisible, matters once structural factors are accounted for.

Repression, political violence, and state failure

I examine how governance in autocracies relates to terrorism and violence, and how state capacity and coercion interact in settings where states weaken or fail.

Language, meaning, and framing

Through corpus methods I trace how words acquire evaluative meaning and how that meaning circulates in fiction and news. The longer-term aim is to understand framing in contemporary media and geopolitics.

Online extremism, recruitment, and moderation evasion

I am exploring how online extremist movements recruit and radicalize, and how the language they use evolves to evade content moderation. I am interested in whether the tools I am building for semantic prosody can help trace how coded terms acquire meaning and spread over time.

Thesis

Master's Thesis

Dividing the Indivisible: Modeling Nuclear Issue-Indivisibility Within Negotiations

Master of International Affairs, Middle Eastern Studies concentration · Penn State · 2026

Does perceived issue indivisibility predict negotiation failure, or do structural factors such as regime type and power asymmetry do the work? I test this with ordered logistic regression on 30 post-Cold War nuclear negotiations.

Current Work

Semantic Prosody Detection in Historical Corpora In development

ARL Walker Graduate Assistantship · Fall 2026 – present

I am developing a Python algorithm to detect semantic prosody, using historical corpora of 18th- and 19th-century Gothic fiction and British newspapers. The goal is to trace how evaluative meaning formed and circulated during the rise of the British Empire, with a tool that can adapt to other languages and to contemporary media framing.
